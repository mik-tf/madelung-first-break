**Abstract.** In the Demkov-Ostrovsky (Tietz) field the one-electron levels with equal $N=n+l$ reach zero energy together, and our earlier papers [P1, P2] showed that a convex departure from it splits each group with lower $n$ first. Here we study the Thomas-Fermi (TF) field, whose chemical potential is zero, so that a level reaching zero energy is a subshell that starts to fill. Theorem A: the zero-energy thresholds of all 30 levels with $N\le10$ are in exactly the Madelung order. Theorem B: the first break is a single transposition, $7h$ ($N=12$) is bound before $11s$ ($N=11$) near $Z=310$, and it is the only inversion below $8g$. Both are proved with interval arithmetic at 640 bits, validated Taylor integration, Sturm comparison and an explicit tail lemma for the $144/s^3$ asymptote. They concern the pure TF zero-energy model, the leading-order large-$Z$ mean field of [BFHS25], giving, to our knowledge, the first certified answer to the question of how large an atom must be before the Madelung rule fails inside that model. A double-precision table of 240 levels up to $N=30$ shows overlaps of consecutive groups for every $N$ from 11 to 29, a semiclassical law with $\beta=1.032$ predicts the first overlap at $N=11$, and along the path from the Tietz to the TF field the first overlap appears at $N=11$ to $16$. A $(Z-1)/Z$ Fermi-Amaldi rescaling keeps the orderings, moving every threshold by about $3/2$ in $Z$, whereas fields with a $-1/r$ tail reorder levels from $N=3$ to $7$, so the result is not a statement about real atoms; the subshell capacities remain an input.

**Keywords.** Madelung rule; Thomas-Fermi equation; zero-energy threshold; interval arithmetic; Sturm comparison; computer-assisted proof.

# Introduction

The Madelung rule says that subshells fill in order of increasing $N=n+l$, and that subshells with equal $N$ fill in order of increasing $n$. We ask a precise question. In the Thomas-Fermi (TF) model of the neutral atom, in what order do the subshells start to fill, and where does the $n+l$ rule first fail?

The two earlier papers of this series treat the model of Demkov and Ostrovsky [DO71, DO72], in which every group of fixed $N$ is exactly degenerate. Paper 1 [P1] identifies that model with the round four-sphere. Paper 2 [P2] shows that a convex perturbation of it lifts the degeneracy with lower $n$ first. Neither paper says what happens between groups. For a field far from the sphere the groups are no longer exactly flat, and one expects that for large $N$ a group reaches into the next. The question is where.

Bjerg, Fournais, Hearnshaw and Solovej [BFHS25] prove that for the non-relativistic many-body atom the large-$Z$ limit of the angular momentum distribution of the electrons is a density $\kappa$ built from the Thomas-Fermi potential, and that it differs from the density $\kappa_M$ the Madelung rule gives. Their result is a statement about the limit $Z\to\infty$. It shows that the rule must fail for some large atoms. It does not say for which $Z$, for which pair of subshells, or by how much. Their last paragraph leaves open how large atoms need to be before the rule no longer applies. We take up the "how large" question inside the pure Thomas-Fermi zero-energy model, the leading-order large-$Z$ mean field in which their density $\kappa$ lives. This is a one-body model, simpler than their many-body setting and concrete enough to give numbers. Our answer is an answer inside that model only (see "What we do not claim").

**What we prove.** Write $G(n,l)$ for the zero-energy threshold of the level $(n,l)$ (Section 2). We prove the following, with the code in `code/` producing certified enclosures.

- The thresholds of all 30 levels with $N\le 10$ are in exactly the Madelung order (Theorem A).
- No group with $N\le 10$ overlaps the next, and the first break of the $n+l$ rule is the transposition of $11s$ and $7h$, near $Z=310$ (Theorem B).
- Two structural lemmas that make the certificate work: a tail lemma for the $144/s^3$ asymptote of the TF function (Lemma 2) and a monotonicity lemma in $n_r$ and $l$ (Lemma 4).

**What is known and what is new.** The first appearance of each angular momentum $l$ in a Thomas-Fermi (TF) atom follows, to leading order, $Z_l\approx 0.155\,(2l+1)^3$ (0.1564 with the constants of Section 2). The closed form $Z_l=\tfrac16(2l+1)^3$ and the filling of the $(n+l)$ groups between $(n+l)^3/6$ and $(n+l+1)^3/6$ were derived in the Tietz-type approximation to the TF field by Klechkovskii [Kle62] (full text of the 1962 note read), and the same leading law is used by Abrahamson [Abr71], who gives $Z=1,5,21,58$ for $l=0$ to 3 and $122,222,367$ for $l=4,5,6$ (abstract and reference list only, full text not consulted). Wong [Won79] gives a theoretical justification of Madelung's rule (full text not consulted). Kitagawara and Barut [KB83] and Ostrovsky [Ost81, Ost01, Ost03] study the Demkov-Ostrovsky model and its symmetry, which is the model of our papers 1 and 2 and not a computation in the exact TF field (abstracts only for [KB83, Ost81], full text not consulted for [Ost01, Ost03]). Belokolos [Bel17] notes that the exact TF field yields only an approximation to the $n+l$ rule, and [BFHS25] prove that for large $Z$ the angular momentum distribution of the TF atom differs from Madelung's. Latter [Lat55] solved the Schrödinger equation in the TF and TF-Dirac potentials for the levels $1s$ to $7d$, with a potential modified to have the asymptotics of a unit charge (abstract only); Senatore and March [SM85] give WKB eigenvalues in the TF potential at large $Z$ (abstract only), and ten Hoor [tH86] studies first-appearance values of subshells in TF theory (abstract and reference list only). The Abrahamson values are $\lceil(2l+1)^3/6\rceil$, his abstract formula $Z_1(l)>(2l+1)^3/6$, and lie 9, 7 and 5 percent below our certified first $g$, $h$ and $i$ ($134.29$, $238.28$, $385.49$), so they are not exact-field thresholds. His full text and ten Hoor [tH86] were not consulted (public sources only). We did not see the tables of [Lat55, Abr71, SM85, tH86], and they may contain numbers close to ours. Numbers for the lowest levels, for the first $g$, $h$ and $i$ subshells, and the leading-order law may therefore already exist, and our values for them should be read as a certified, higher-precision version, not as first values. What we find in none of the sources we could access, and what we claim, is (a) interval-arithmetic brackets of the zero-energy thresholds of all subshells with $n+l\le 12$ in the exact TF field (Table C), (b) a proof that groups 1 to 10 do not overlap their successors and that $11s$ is certified above $7h$ (Theorem B(i), (ii)), and (c) the uniqueness statement that $(11s,7h)$ is the only inversion among all levels below $8g$ (Theorem B(iii)), so that the first breaking pair is $11s$ and $7h$ at $Z$ between 309 and 313. Novelty rests on the certified proof and on this uniqueness, not on the numbers being unseen. The Tietz-type derivation of Klechkovskii is exactly $n+l$ by construction and does not apply to the exact TF field; the works that use the exact field, as far as we could read, do not state a pair of subshells that is out of order. We do not claim that the $n+l$ rule fails in the TF atom in general: [BFHS25] and [Bel17] already point that way at large $Z$; our new content is the certified first failure.

**What we do not claim.** The theorems are about the pure TF zero-energy model: the one-body operator with the field $\varphi_{\rm TF}$, in which a threshold is the coupling at which a level reaches zero energy. This is a leading-order, neutral, non-relativistic mean field, not a self-consistent atom: filling every negative level does not give $Z$ electrons. A value such as $Z=310$ is far beyond any physical atom. We make no claim about real atoms or about corrected mean fields (exchange, Scott and relativistic terms, or the $-1/r$ tail of Latter [Lat55]). The decisive margins are small, 0.8 percent in the coupling between $7h$ and $11s$ and $4.4\times10^{-3}$ between groups 10 and 11, and we claim no robustness of them under such corrections: Remark I shows numerically that fields with a $-1/r$ tail reorder levels at much smaller $N$. We do not derive the subshell capacities $2(2l+1)$; the factor 2 and the factor $2l+1$ are put in by hand. Our statements are about the single function $\varphi_{\rm TF}$.

**Roadmap.** Section 2 sets up the problem. Section 3 describes the certificate method. Section 4 states the lemmas and theorems and proves them by pointing to the certificates. The sections on the table to $N\le 30$ and the comparison with measured ground states follow Theorem B. The section on the spread and gap law derives the semiclassical spread and gap law and relates it to the density $\kappa$ of [BFHS25]. Remark I, at the end of Section 4, reports the numerical test of how specific the orderings are to the pure TF field.

# Setup

**The TF function.** Let $\varphi=\varphi_{\rm TF}$ solve
$$\varphi''(s)=\frac{\varphi(s)^{3/2}}{\sqrt s},\qquad \varphi(0)=1,\qquad \varphi(s)\to 0\ (s\to\infty).$$
It is positive, decreasing and convex. Its initial slope is $\varphi'(0)=B^*=-1.588071\ldots$, and for large $s$ it is bounded by the exact solution $\psi(s)=144/s^3$ of the same equation. The next term of the large-$s$ expansion is the Coulson-March correction [CM50].

**The zero-energy problem.** In the scaled variable $s$ the radial equation at energy zero for angular momentum $l$ and coupling $G>0$ is
$$u''(s)=\Bigl[\frac{L}{s^2}-\frac{G\,\varphi(s)}{s}\Bigr]u(s),\qquad L=l(l+1),\tag{2.1}$$
with $u$ regular at $s=0$. Let $Z_l(G)$ be the number of zeros of the regular solution on $(0,\infty)$. By Sturm theory $Z_l(G)$ is non-decreasing in $G$. The zero-energy threshold of the level $(n,l)$, with $n_r=n-l-1$ the number of radial nodes, is
$$G(n,l)=\inf\{G:\ Z_l(G)\ge n-l\}.$$

**The model, and why a threshold is a first filling.** What is computed is the coupling at which the one-body operator $-\Delta-Z\varphi_{\rm TF}(r/b)/r$ (in scaled form, (2.1)) acquires its $(n_r+1)$-th negative level of angular momentum $l$. This is the one-electron model of the central-field treatments of Fermi, Latter and Abrahamson [Lat55, Abr71], but it is not a self-consistent atom: filling every negative level does not give $Z$ electrons. The notion of a zero-energy threshold is specific to the pure TF field, which decays like $s^{-3}$ and so gives each $l$ finitely many bound levels. With the $-1/r$ tail of the Latter correction [Lat55], every $l$ has infinitely many bound levels and zero energy is no longer a threshold (Remark I). In the neutral TF atom the chemical potential is zero. A level with negative energy is occupied, so a level whose energy reaches zero as the coupling grows is a subshell that starts to fill. The coupling $G$ grows with the atomic number as
$$G=2\,(0.885341\ldots)\,Z^{2/3}=1.770682\ldots\,Z^{2/3},$$
where $0.885341\ldots=\tfrac12(3\pi/4)^{2/3}$ is the TF length scale. So $Z_{\rm first}(n,l)=\bigl(G(n,l)/1.770682\ldots\bigr)^{3/2}$, and the order of the thresholds is the order in which subshells start to fill. This is a statement inside the model.

**The Bessel tail match.** For $s$ large, $\varphi\approx 144/s^3$ and (2.1) becomes, with $x=1/s$ and $u=s\,v$, a Bessel equation of index $l+\tfrac12$ in $12\sqrt G\,x$. The two solutions are $u=s^{1/2}J_{\pm(l+1/2)}(12\sqrt G/s)$, with $J_{-(l+1/2)}\sim x^{-(l+1/2)}$ giving $s^{l+1}$ and $J_{l+1/2}\sim x^{l+1/2}$ giving $s^{-l}$ as $x=12\sqrt G/s\to0$. The solution that is regular at $0$ therefore behaves as $u\approx b\,s^{l+1}+c\,s^{-l}$ at infinity. The threshold is a root of the growing amplitude, $b(G)=0$; at such a root $u$ tends to a constant for $l=0$ (a zero-energy resonance) and decays like $s^{-l}$ for $l\ge1$.

**Lemma 1 (threshold characterisation).** Let $H_l(G)=-\frac{d^2}{ds^2}+\frac{L}{s^2}-\frac{G\varphi(s)}{s}$ on $L^2(0,\infty)$. For every $(n,l)$, with $n_r=n-l-1$:
(a) $Z_l(G(n,l))=n-l-1$, and $Z_l(G)\ge n-l$ for $G>G(n,l)$;
(b) $G(n,l)$ is the $(n-l)$-th positive root of $b(G)=0$;
(c) the $(n_r+1)$-th eigenvalue of $H_l(G)$ is negative if and only if $G>G(n,l)$.

*Proof.* The integer $Z_l(G)$ is non-decreasing in $G$ and changes only when a zero enters from infinity, that is at a root of $b$ (variable phase approach, Calogero [Cal67]). At such a root the solution still has the zeros counted before the jump, since the new one is at infinity. With the definition of $G(n,l)$ this gives (a) and (b), provided every root of $b$ is simple, so that each root raises $Z_l$ by exactly one. This holds: at a root, $\partial_G b\neq0$, because the Wronskian of $u$ with $\partial_G u$ gives $\partial_G b\propto\int_0^\infty\varphi\,u^2/s\,ds>0$, and the integral converges for the $144/s^3$ tail, also for $l=0$ at the resonance. For $G$ not a root of $b$, the number of negative eigenvalues of $H_l(G)$ equals $Z_l(G)$ (Sturm oscillation theory for potentials that decay faster than $s^{-2}$, Reed and Simon [RS78]; the precise theorem number is not given here). So the $(n_r+1)$-th eigenvalue is negative if and only if $Z_l(G)\ge n-l$, which by (a) holds if and only if $G>G(n,l)$. At $G=G(n,l)$ the level is at zero energy. $\square$

*Role of Lemma 1.* The certificate (Section 3, Theorems A and B, Lemma 4) uses only the definition of $G(n,l)$ and the monotonicity of $Z_l$ in $G$. Lemma 1 is used for the physical reading of a threshold as a level reaching zero energy (this section and Corollary C).

# The certificate method

We want to prove inequalities between thresholds, not to approximate them. So we bracket each threshold between two values $G_{\rm lo}<G_{\rm hi}$ using zero counts of (2.1), and every count is a validated computation. The code is in `code/`; all arithmetic is interval arithmetic with outward rounding at 640 bits.

**Brackets from zero counts.** A bracket of a level is $[G_{\rm num}(1-\delta),\,G_{\rm num}(1+\delta)]$, where $G_{\rm num}$ is a double-precision estimate used only as the centre and $\delta$ is $10^{-3}$ or $10^{-4}$ (Section 4). Since $Z_l$ is non-decreasing in $G$, the two conditions $Z_l(G_{\rm lo})\le n-l-1$ and $Z_l(G_{\rm hi})\ge n-l$ imply $G_{\rm lo}\le G(n,l)\le G_{\rm hi}$. The order test between two levels $A$ before $B$ is the strict inequality $G_{\rm hi}(A)<G_{\rm lo}(B)$.

**A sandwich for $\varphi$.** We do not know $\varphi$ exactly, so we enclose it. For the initial slope $B$, the solution $\varphi_B$ of the TF equation with $\varphi_B(0)=1$ is either driven to zero (then $B<B^*$) or turns upward (then $B>B^*$). The energy $E=\tfrac12\varphi'^2-\tfrac25\varphi^{5/2}/\sqrt s$ satisfies $E'=\tfrac15\varphi^{5/2}s^{-3/2}>0$, so $E>0$ with $\varphi'<0$ forces a zero. We take the slopes $B_0\mp 10^{-35}$ around the 40-digit value $B_0$ of $B^*$ and certify, by validated stepping, that one solution hits zero and the other turns up. This gives validated functions $\varphi_{\rm lo}<\varphi_{\rm TF}<\varphi_{\rm hi}$ for $s\le 1.451\times 10^{5}$ (Lemma 3).

**Validated Taylor steps.** Equation (2.1) and the TF equation are integrated together by Taylor series of order 50 with relative steps of 3 percent, with a Lagrange remainder on an a priori box obtained by a Gronwall estimate. A step is accepted only if the enclosure of $u$ is bounded away from zero, or if $u'$ has one sign and the end values have certified opposite signs. Otherwise the code stops. If a zero cannot be isolated, the step is refined to 1 percent, then 0.3 percent, with its own sandwich. In the shipped run of the overlap certificate the 1 percent step is used for four levels ($11s$, $9p$, $12s$, $7i$); the 0.3 percent step exists in the code but never ran.

**Lemma S (start data).** Let $s_0=(0.03)^2$ and let $\bar u$, $\underline u$ be the regular solutions of (2.1) with $\varphi$ replaced by $\bar\varphi=1$ and by $\underline\varphi=1-1.589\,s$, with Prüfer angles $\bar\theta,\underline\theta$ (defined as in Lemma 4). Let $\theta$ be the angle of the regular solution $u$ for $\varphi_{\rm TF}$. Then (i) $\underline\varphi\le\varphi_{\rm TF}\le\bar\varphi$ on $[0,\infty)$; (ii) $\underline\theta\le\theta\le\bar\theta$ on $(0,s_0]$; (iii) $\bar u$ has no zero on $(0,s_0]$, so $u$ has none either.

*Proof.* (i) $\varphi_{\rm TF}$ is decreasing with $\varphi(0)=1$, so $\varphi\le1$; it is convex, so $\varphi(s)\ge1+B^*s\ge1-1.589\,s$, because $B^*=-1.588071\ldots\ge-1.589$. (ii) Let $a,b$ be two of the three problems with $\varphi_a\ge\varphi_b$, so that $q_a=L/s^2-G\varphi_a/s\le q_b$. The Coulomb singularity does not change the leading behaviour: both regular solutions satisfy $u=s^{l+1}(1+o(1))$ and $u'=(l+1)s^l(1+o(1))$ at $0^+$. Hence $W=u_au_b'-u_a'u_b=\rho_a\rho_b\sin(\theta_a-\theta_b)$ is $o(s^{2l+1})$, so $W(0^+)=0$, and $W'=(q_b-q_a)u_au_b\ge0$ while both solutions are positive, which holds on some $(0,s_1]$. So $W>0$ there, and both angles lie in $(0,\pi/2)$, which gives $\theta_a>\theta_b$ on $(0,s_1]$. Beyond $s_1$ the Prüfer equation has a right side that is non-decreasing in $\varphi$, and the comparison theorem for first-order equations carries the strict order to all $s>0$. Applied to $\bar\varphi\ge\varphi_{\rm TF}\ge\underline\varphi$ this is (ii). (iii) The power series of $\bar u$ and its explicit tail bound show $\bar u>0$ on $(0,s_0]$, so $\bar\theta<\pi$ there, hence $\theta<\pi$ by (ii), and $u$ has no zero. $\square$

In the code the angles at $s_0$ come from the Coulomb problems $u''=(L/s^2-G/s+c)u$ with $c=0$ (this is $\bar\varphi$, the larger angle, used for the lower bracket) and $c=1.589\,G$ (this is $\underline\varphi$, the smaller angle, used for the upper bracket), each as an entire power series with a tail bound.

**Upper bracket.** With the potential $\varphi_{\rm lo}$, which has fewer zeros than the true one, the code counts $n-l$ certified sign changes of $u$ before the end of the validated range. Hence $Z_l(G_{\rm hi})\ge n-l$. The deciding zeros for all $l=0$ levels with $N\le12$ lie at $s\le 59769$, a factor 2.4 inside the validated range, so no extension was needed. Each finer sandwich has its own, slightly shorter range ($s\le1.433\times10^5$ at 1 percent), which the code enforces.

**Lower bracket and the tail lemma.** With the potential $\varphi_{\rm hi}$ the code counts exactly $k=n-l-1$ certified zeros on $(0,S]$, $S=4900$. Zeros after $S$ are excluded analytically.

**Lemma 2 (tail lemma).** Let $\psi(s)=144/s^3$. Then $\varphi\le\psi$ on $(0,\infty)$; in particular $\varphi(S)\le\psi(S)$ holds for every $S$, with no check needed. Let $Q(s)=L/s^2-G\varphi(s)/s$ and $w=u'/u$ at $S$. If $w(S)\ge a^*/S$, where $a^*$ is the lower root of $a^2-a=L-144\,G/S^2$ (the roots are real: $1+4(L-144G/S^2)\ge0$, true for every level used, since $576\,G<S^2$), then $u$ has no zero after $S$. The same holds for the solution $\tilde u$ of the comparison equation $\tilde u''=[L/s^2-G\psi/s]\tilde u$ on $[S,\infty)$ with $\tilde w(S)\ge a^*/S$; the Riccati part of the proof below is for this equation, to which the case of $\varphi$ reduces since $\varphi\le\psi$.

*Proof.* The function $\psi$ solves the TF equation exactly. Put $d=\varphi-\psi$. Then $d''=(\varphi^{3/2}-\psi^{3/2})/\sqrt s$ has the sign of $d$. Suppose $d>0$ somewhere and take a component $(\alpha,\beta)$ of $\{d>0\}$. Since $d(0^+)=-\infty$, $\alpha>0$; on the component $d$ is convex with $d(\alpha)=0$. If $\beta<\infty$ then $d(\beta)=0$ and convexity gives $d\le 0$, a contradiction. If $\beta=\infty$ then $d\to 0$ at infinity, which a convex function that starts at $0$ and is positive cannot do. So $\varphi\le\psi$ on $(0,\infty)$. Then $Q\ge L/s^2-144G/s^4$ on $[S,\infty)$. The function $g=a^*/s$ satisfies $g'+g^2\le L/s^2-144G/s^4$ for $s\ge S$, so by Riccati comparison $w\ge g$ on $[S,\infty)$ as long as $w$ exists; since $g$ is finite, $w$ cannot tend to $-\infty$, so $u$ has no zero there. $\square$

As a consistency check of the enclosure, the code verifies $\varphi_{\rm hi}(S)\le\psi(S)$ at $S=4900$ (the ratio is $0.9814$; the N12 log prints only "tail hypothesis True"). The check that matters is $w_{\rm hi}(S)>a^*/S$, made for every level.

**Sturm comparison.** The Prüfer angle $\theta$ of $u$ ($u=\rho\sin\theta$, $u'=\rho\cos\theta$) satisfies $\theta'=\cos^2\theta+(G\varphi/s-L/s^2)\sin^2\theta$. The right side is non-decreasing in $\varphi$ and non-increasing in $L$. The comparison starts at $s_0$, not at $0$: by Lemma S the true angle at $s_0$ lies between $\underline\theta(s_0)$ and $\bar\theta(s_0)$. For the lower bracket the code integrates from $\bar\theta(s_0)$ with $\varphi_{\rm hi}$, and for the upper bracket from $\underline\theta(s_0)$ with $\varphi_{\rm lo}$. Since a larger start angle and a larger $\varphi$ both give a larger angle, comparison on $[s_0,S]$ gives $\theta_{\rm true}\le\theta_{\rm hi}$ for the run at $G_{\rm lo}$ and $\theta_{\rm lo}\le\theta_{\rm true}$ for the run at $G_{\rm hi}$ (each bound is used only at its own coupling), so for the upper bracket $\theta_{\rm true}(S)\le\theta_{\rm hi}(S)<(k+1)\pi$. On $[S,\infty)$ let $\tilde\theta$ solve the Prüfer equation with $\varphi$ replaced by $\psi=144/s^3$ and $\tilde\theta(S)=\theta_{\rm hi}(S)$. Since $\varphi_{\rm TF}\le\psi$ there (Lemma 2) and $\theta_{\rm true}(S)\le\tilde\theta(S)$, comparison gives $\theta_{\rm true}\le\tilde\theta$ on $[S,\infty)$. By Lemma 2 applied to $\tilde\theta$, whose cotangent at $S$ is $w_{\rm hi}(S)\ge a^*/S$, $\tilde\theta$ never reaches $(k+1)\pi$. Hence the true solution has at most $k$ zeros on $(0,\infty)$, and $Z_l(G_{\rm lo})\le k$.

**What is computer-checked and what is on paper.** Interval computations: the sandwich, the Taylor steps with remainders, the zero counts, the start data, the tail hypothesis, the Riccati criterion. On paper, with standard theorems: Lemma S (the Wronskian ordering at $0^+$), Sturm comparison of Prüfer angles, monotonicity of $Z_l$ in $G$, $\varphi_{\rm TF}(\infty)=0$, and Lemma 1. The rigour rests on the outward rounding of the interval library. A cross-check with a second library was made with Arb ball arithmetic (python-flint, 400 bits, Taylor order 36), with new code that imports nothing from the first one. For nine levels ($7h$, $11s$, $8g$, $8h$, $7i$, $10p$, $2p$, $3d$, $5f$) it reproduces the sign and the zero count at both ends of the bracket, and the sandwich and the tail check pass too. Its log shows, for each of the nine levels, its own start data, the sandwich, the tail hypothesis $y(70)<144/70^6$ and the zero count and sign at both ends. The cross-check covers these nine levels, not the whole table. The brackets do not depend on the tabulated centres, which are only starting guesses.

**Where the computations ran.** All computations are CPU-only Python with mpmath interval arithmetic at 640 bits, one process on one thread, with no GPU. The overlap certificate for $N\le 12$ (the 42 levels with $N\le12$ and the two group 13 levels $7i$, $8h$) took 6920 s, about 115 minutes, on a machine under heavy load. The double-precision table to $N=30$ took 3529 s, plus 82 s for a patch run of the $l=0$ levels. The logs do not record library versions; the fast path of `code/run_all.py` was last run with Python 3.12.3, mpmath 1.2.1, numpy 1.26.4, scipy 1.11.4, sympy 1.12 and matplotlib 3.6.3. The code and the logs are in code/, including `code/tf_wkb.py` (about 30 s, light), which prints every number of Proposition E2.

# Results

**Lemma 3 (TF sandwich).** Let $B_0$ be the 40-digit value of the initial slope used in `code/`, and $B_\mp=B_0\mp10^{-35}$. The solution with slope $B_-$ is certified to reach zero and the one with slope $B_+$ to turn upward, so $B_-<B^*<B_+$. The solutions $\varphi_{\rm lo},\varphi_{\rm hi}$ with these slopes satisfy $\varphi_{\rm lo}<\varphi_{\rm TF}<\varphi_{\rm hi}$ on $(0,1.451\times10^5]$.

*Proof.* The first part is the energy argument of Section 3 with validated stepping. Since the right side $\varphi^{3/2}/\sqrt s$ is increasing in $\varphi$, solutions with equal initial value are ordered like their initial slopes while they are positive. $\square$

**Lemma 4 (monotonicity in $n_r$ and $l$).** Write $G(n_r,l)$ for $G(n,l)$ with $n=n_r+l+1$, so that $G(n_r,l)=\inf\{G>0:\ Z_l(G)\ge n_r+1\}$. Then $G(n_r,l)\le G(n_r+1,l)$ and $G(n_r,l)\le G(n_r,l+1)$ for all $n_r,l\ge 0$. Hence $G(n_r',l')\le G(n_r,l)$ whenever $n_r'\le n_r$ and $l'\le l$.

*Proof.* In $n_r$: $Z_l(G)\ge n_r+2$ implies $Z_l(G)\ge n_r+1$, so the set defining $G(n_r+1,l)$ lies inside the set defining $G(n_r,l)$, and its infimum is not smaller.

In $l$: fix $G$. Since $G\varphi(s)$ is bounded near $0$, the regular solution of (2.1) satisfies $u_l=s^{l+1}(1+o(1))$ and $u_l'=(l+1)s^l(1+o(1))$ as $s\to0^+$ [Cal67]. Let $\theta_l$ be its Prüfer angle ($u_l=\rho_l\sin\theta_l$, $u_l'=\rho_l\cos\theta_l$, $\rho_l>0$), taken in $(0,\pi/2)$ near $0$, so $\theta_l(0^+)=0$ and $\theta_l'=f_l(s,\theta_l)$ with $f_l(s,\theta)=\cos^2\theta+\bigl(G\varphi/s-l(l+1)/s^2\bigr)\sin^2\theta$. The equation is singular at $s=0$, so we first order the angles near $0$. The Wronskian $W=u_lu_{l+1}'-u_l'u_{l+1}=\rho_l\rho_{l+1}\sin(\theta_l-\theta_{l+1})$ satisfies $W'=2(l+1)\,u_lu_{l+1}/s^2$ and $W=O(s^{2l+2})\to0$. Choose $s_1>0$ such that $u_l,u_l',u_{l+1},u_{l+1}'>0$ on $(0,s_1]$. There $W(s)=\int_0^s 2(l+1)u_lu_{l+1}t^{-2}\,dt>0$, and both angles lie in $(0,\pi/2)$, so $\theta_{l+1}<\theta_l$ on $(0,s_1]$. On $[s_1,\infty)$, $f_l$ is continuous and Lipschitz in $\theta$ on compact $s$-intervals, and $f_{l+1}(s,\theta)=f_l(s,\theta)-\tfrac{2(l+1)}{s^2}\sin^2\theta\le f_l(s,\theta)$, so the comparison theorem for first-order equations gives $\theta_{l+1}(s)<\theta_l(s)$ for all $s>0$. At $\theta\in\pi\mathbb Z$ the equation gives $\theta'=1$, so each angle crosses every multiple of $\pi$ once and upward, and the number of zeros of $u_l$ on $(0,X]$ is $\lfloor\theta_l(X)/\pi\rfloor$. Hence the count for $l+1$ on $(0,X]$ is at most the count for $l$, for every $X$, and $Z_{l+1}(G)\le Z_l(G)$. So $\{G:Z_{l+1}(G)\ge n_r+1\}\subseteq\{G:Z_l(G)\ge n_r+1\}$ and $G(n_r,l+1)\ge G(n_r,l)$. The last claim follows by repeating the two unit steps. Only the definition of $G(n,l)$ in Section 2 is used: not Lemma 1, not the behaviour at infinity, and no continuity in a real $l$. $\square$

**Theorem A (Madelung order).** The thresholds of all 30 levels with $N\le 10$ are in exactly the Madelung order: $N$ first, and lower $n$ first within $N$. Equivalently, in the model of Section 2 the subshells $1s$ to $10s$ start to fill in the Madelung order, up to $Z\approx 237$ (Corollary C: $Z_{\rm first}(10s)\in[236.65,236.73]$).

*Proof.* The overlap certificate (brackets $G_{\rm num}(1\pm10^{-4})$, relative width $2\times10^{-4}$) re-certifies all 42 levels of groups 1 to 12. Its check (a) gives $G_{\rm hi}(i)<G_{\rm lo}(i+1)$ for every consecutive pair inside a group, for all 42 levels, so each group is in Madelung order. Its check (b) compares the maximal $G_{\rm hi}$ (the level $l=0$) of group $N$ with the minimal $G_{\rm lo}$ (the level $l=l_{\max}$) of group $N+1$, for every boundary $N=1,\dots,10$; this comparison is valid only together with (a), and then gives Theorem B(i). With (a) and B(i), transitivity gives the order of all 30 levels with $N\le10$. The earlier certificate for $N\le 8$ (brackets $G_{\rm num}(1\pm10^{-3})$) gives, independently, 20 pairwise disjoint brackets in Madelung order, with consecutive centres at least $2.97\times10^{-2}$ apart in relative size (the brackets themselves at least $2.77\times10^{-2}$ apart). $\square$

**Theorem B (first break).**

(i) For every $N\le10$, every level of group $N$ lies below every level of group $N+1$.

(ii) $G(11s)>G(7h)$.

An inversion is a pair $(A,B)$ with $A$ before $B$ in Madelung order and $G(A)>G(B)$.

(iii) $(11s,7h)$ is the only inversion among levels with $G<G(8g)$, and in fact the only inversion in which at least one member has $G<G(8g)$.

*Proof.* (i) and (ii) are conditions of the overlap certificate. The tightest case of (i) is $N=10$ to $11$: $G_{\rm hi}(10s)=67.75945<G_{\rm lo}(6h)=68.05600$, a relative gap of $4.4\times10^{-3}$. For (ii), $G_{\rm lo}(11s)=81.58683>G_{\rm hi}(7h)=80.94905$, a gap of 0.8 percent. In $Z$ this puts the first break at $Z$ between 309 and 313: from the brackets, $Z_{\rm first}(7h)\in[309.01,309.11]$ and $Z_{\rm first}(11s)\in[312.76,312.86]$.

For (iii), the code asserts the strict inequalities $10p<7h$, $11s<8g$, $7h<8g$, $8g<9f$, $8g<7i$, $8g<8h$ in the sense $G_{\rm hi}(x)<G_{\rm lo}(y)$. Let $S$ be the set of levels with $G<G(8g)$. Groups 1 to 11 are in Madelung order among themselves: inside groups 1 to 10 by Theorem A, inside group 11 by the in-group order of the overlap certificate, and across groups by (i) and transitivity. Since $11s<8g$ and $11s$ is the top of group 11, all of groups 1 to 11 lie in $S$. Of group 12 only $7h$ lies in $S$, since $7h<8g<9f<10d<11p<12s$. No level with $N\ge13$ lies in $S$: from $N=n_r+2l+1$ we get $n_r+2l\ge12$, and with $l'=\min(l,6)$, $n_r'=12-2l'$ we have $n_r'\le n_r$ and $l'\le l$, so by Lemma 4 the threshold of $(n_r,l)$ is at least that of the group 13 level $(n_r',l')$, one of $13s,12p,11d,10f,9g,8h,7i$. The first five are at least $G(12s),G(11p),G(10d),G(9f),G(8g)$ respectively by Lemma 4 in $n_r$, hence at least $G(8g)$; $8h$ and $7i$ are certified directly. So $S$ consists of groups 1 to 11 and $7h$, and $7h$ comes last in Madelung order. Every level $A\ne 11s$ of groups 1 to 11 has $G(A)\le G(10p)<G(7h)$, while $G(11s)>G(7h)$ by (ii). Hence $(11s,7h)$ is the only inversion in $S$. Finally let $(A,B)$ be an inversion with a member in $S$. If $A\in S$ then $G(B)<G(A)<G(8g)$, so $B\in S$. If $B\in S$, then $A$, which comes before $B$ in Madelung order, lies in groups 1 to 11 or is earlier than $7h$ in group 12 (there is none), so $A\in S$. Thus both members lie in $S$, and the pair is $(11s,7h)$. $\square$

**Corollary C (Z ranges).** Let $Z(G)=\left(G/1.770682\ldots\right)^{3/2}=4G^{3/2}/(3\pi)$, where $1.770682\ldots=(3\pi/4)^{2/3}$ is twice the Thomas-Fermi constant $0.885341\ldots$. For every subshell with $N\le12$ the atomic number $Z_{\rm first}$ at which, in the model of Section 2, it first fills lies in the interval of Table C. The intervals are rounded outward to two decimals (lower ends down, upper ends up). For $N\le10$ they are pairwise disjoint and in Madelung order. In particular $Z_{\rm first}(10s)\in[236.65,\,236.73]$, $Z_{\rm first}(7h)\in[309.01,\,309.11]$, $Z_{\rm first}(11s)\in[312.76,\,312.86]$ and $Z_{\rm first}(8g)\in[317.34,\,317.44]$.

*Proof.* In the model of Section 2 the chemical potential is zero, so the subshell $(n,l)$ starts to fill when its level reaches zero energy, that is at the coupling $G=G(n,l)$, and $G$ is related to the nuclear charge by $G=1.770682\ldots\,Z^{2/3}$. The map $Z\mapsto G$ is a strictly increasing bijection of $(0,\infty)$, with the strictly increasing inverse $G\mapsto Z(G)$. Hence if the certificate gives $G_{\rm lo}\le G(n,l)\le G_{\rm hi}$, then $Z(G_{\rm lo})\le Z_{\rm first}(n,l)\le Z(G_{\rm hi})$. The brackets are those of the certificates for $N\le12$ (relative width $2\times10^{-4}$), and $Z(G_{\rm lo})$, $Z(G_{\rm hi})$ are evaluated in interval arithmetic with outward rounding, with the printed centres $G_{\rm num}$ widened by $10^{-9}$ in relative size to cover their ten-digit rounding. The intervals obtained from the coarser $N\le8$ certificate (relative width $2\times10^{-3}$) contain these, as the script checks. Since $Z(\cdot)$ is increasing, the strict inequalities $G_{\rm hi}(i)<G_{\rm lo}(j)$ of Theorems A and B give $Z_{\rm hi}(i)<Z_{\rm lo}(j)$, which is the disjointness and the order. $\square$ The script `code/cor_c_z_ranges.py` and its log are in `code/`; the script prints $Z_{\rm lo}$ and $Z_{\rm hi}$ to 12 digits and the outward-rounded pair, from which Table C is copied.

**Proposition D (numerical, dataset).** The zero-energy couplings $G(n,l)$ of all 240 levels with $N\le 30$ were computed in double precision (bracket at $s=200$, integration to $s=10^6$, root from a Chebyshev interpolant). The largest error estimate is $3.6\times10^{-14}$ in relative size, below the required $10^{-9}$, and the 72 rows with $N\le16$ equal the earlier table in `code/tf_threshold_table.csv`, computed by the same method, exactly. As a normalisation check, the ratio of $G$ to the coupling variable $Z$ of [P2] at the Thomas-Fermi end of its path (the threshold coupling of [P2]'s weighted zero-energy problem, in units where the Tietz couplings are $Z_N=N(N+1)/2$) is a constant conversion factor, $1.0600385$ at the levels $2p$, $3s$, $11s$ and $7h$ (here $Z$ is [P2]'s coupling variable, not the atomic number). The ratio agrees at these four levels to a relative spread of $9.3\times10^{-7}$, which is the rounding of the five-decimal values of [P2]. This is numerical evidence, not a certificate.

**Proposition E (numerical, spread).** Let $s_N=G(N,0)/G(N,l_{\max})-1$. Numerically $s_N$ rises slowly ($s_{10}=0.1846$, $s_{20}=0.2168$, $s_{30}=0.2282$), each odd $N$ lies above the next even $N$ for all 14 pairs with $N=3,\dots,30$ (the pair $(1,2)$ has both spreads zero), and the largest gap lies after $ns$ for $N=2$ to $7$ and after $np$ for $N=8$ to $10$ (for $N=8$ the gap is $0.0771$), where the gap after $ns$ is the gap from the top of group $N$ to the bottom of group $N+1$, and the gap after $np$ is the gap between the $l=1$ and $l=0$ levels of group $N$. The parity alternation $A_N=s_{2m+1}-s_{2m+2}$, labelled by the pair containing $N$ (so $A_{10}$ is the pair $9,10$), is an endpoint effect and not a Kelvin parity effect (in [P1, P2], Kelvin parity is the reflection of the four-sphere that exchanges the nucleus pole and the far pole; here the alternation comes from the end levels $ns$ and $n\,l_{\max}$, not from that symmetry): $A_{10}=0.004003$, $A_{20}=0.002927$, $A_{30}=0.002181$, so $A_{30}/A_{10}=0.545$, and a semiclassical (WKB) amplitude with the exact endpoints has the right sign for all pairs and matches the size to within 12 percent for $N\ge10$. For every $N$ from 11 to 29 a level of group $N+1$ lies below the top of group $N$. This last statement is numerical evidence, not a theorem.

**Proposition E2 (semiclassical, derivation at physics rigour, not a theorem).** Write $f(s)=s\varphi(s)$, $\lambda=l+\tfrac12$ and $K(\mu)=\frac1\pi\int(f-\mu^2)_+^{1/2}\,d\ln s$. The Langer-Bohr-Sommerfeld rule at zero energy, $\sqrt G\,K(\lambda/\sqrt G)=n-l-\tfrac12$, gives (the order of the error terms is asserted from the expansion, not proved, and is supported by the residuals below) $G(N,0)=(N+\tfrac14)^2/K(0)^2\,(1+O(N^{-2}\log N))$ and $G(N,l_{\max})=N^2(1+\beta/N)/(4f_{\max})$ for odd $N$, $N^2(1+(3\beta-2)/N)/(4f_{\max})$ for even $N$, with $\beta=\sqrt{2|f''|/f_{\max}}=1.03216$ at the maximum of $f$ in $\ln s$. Hence $s_N=s_\infty+c_\pm/N+O(N^{-2}\log N)$ with $s_\infty=4f_{\max}/K(0)^2-1=0.25209$, $c_{\rm odd}=(1+s_\infty)(\tfrac12-\beta)=-0.666$, $c_{\rm even}=(1+s_\infty)(\tfrac52-3\beta)=-0.747$, and the gap between group starts is $2\beta/N$ ($N$ odd) or $(4-2\beta)/N$ ($N$ even). A least-squares extrapolation of the table gives $s_\infty=0.2519$ (models from 0.2508 to 0.2521), within 0.1 percent; the fitted $1/N$ coefficients agree with $c_\pm$ to about 1 percent, and the residuals of the parameter-free law fall like $N^{-2}$ ($7\times10^{-3}$ at $N=10$, $1\times10^{-3}$ at $N=30$). The overlap condition $s_N>{}$gap holds from $N>(2\beta-c_{\rm odd})/s_\infty=10.8$, so the law predicts $N^*=11$, and the alternation amplitude tends to $(1+s_\infty)(2\beta-2)/N=0.081/N$. On $N\le30$ the measured alternation $A_N$, for the pair $(N-1,N)$, is still a factor 2.1 (at $N=10$) to 1.3 (at $N=30$) below this limit, where the limit $0.081/N$ is evaluated at the midpoint $N-\tfrac12$ of the pair; the agreement within 12 percent quoted in Proposition E is for the WKB amplitude with the exact endpoints, not for the limit. *Relation to [BFHS25].* Their density is $\kappa(\lambda)=c\,K(\lambda/c)$ with $c=(3\pi/4)^{1/3}$ (fixed by $\int4\lambda\kappa=1$), and $G=c^2Z^{2/3}$, so the rule above reads $Z^{1/3}\kappa(\lambda Z^{-1/3})=n-l-\tfrac12$. This is the density of [BFHS25]; what is new here is the spread and gap law derived from it, not the density. The spread limit is a functional of $\kappa$, $1+s_\infty=(2\lambda_c/\kappa(0))^2$ with $\lambda_c=\sup\operatorname{supp}\kappa$; it vanishes for $\kappa_M$. The two ends give $Z_{\rm first}(l)=0.1564\,(2l+1)^3$ for the first $l$ (the leading law of the Introduction) and $Z_{\rm first}(ns)=(n+\tfrac14)^3/\kappa(0)^3$, which gives 312.0 for $11s$ ($11.25^3/1.658653^3$), against the certified $[312.76,312.86]$. A derivation (splitting the integral at the maximum; not a theorem here) gives $\kappa'(0)=-\tfrac32$, under the hypotheses $\varphi(0)=1$, the $144/s^3$ asymptote with relative correction $o(1)$, and $f$ bounded away from $0$ on compact sets, against $\kappa_M'(0)=-2$ for $\kappa_M(\lambda)=(6^{1/3}-2\lambda)_+$: the core contributes $-1$ and the $144/s^3$ tail $-\tfrac12$, where a $1/s^2$ tail would give $-1$. The product $N^*s_\infty$ is not universal across screened fields: along the same family with $\varepsilon=0.25$, $0.5$ and $1$ it is $2.04$, $2.04$ and $2.77$, so the first-overlap estimate above is specific to the Thomas-Fermi field and we claim no field-independent law for $N^*s_\infty$.

**Proposition F (numerical, path onset).** Let the weight field be $s\,\varphi_\varepsilon(s)$ with $\varphi_\varepsilon(s)=(1-\varepsilon)A\,T(s)+\varepsilon\,\varphi_{\rm TF}(s)$, where $T(s)=(1+as)^{-2}$ is the nearest Tietz field of [P2] in the weighted $L^2$ norm (weight $s$ on the half line, amplitude free; $a=0.536795$, $A=1.012783$, as in [P2]). This family is the family $w(1+\varepsilon g_{\rm TF})$ of [P2] up to the constant $A$ in the coupling; the code labels it by that name. The path is defined by, so that $\varepsilon=0$ is the Tietz field and $\varepsilon=1$ the Thomas-Fermi field. Numerically, in double precision (a grid in $\varepsilon$ refined where two levels come within $10^{-3}$), the first cross-group overlap along this path appears for $N=11$ to $16$ at $\varepsilon^*_N=0.9576,\,0.8506,\,0.8090,\,0.7381,\,0.7086,\,0.6564$. The product $N\varepsilon^*_N$ stays between $10.21$ and $10.63$, a spread of 4 percent. We draw no further conclusion from it: that the onset scales like $1/N$, or like $1/\varepsilon$, is not derived, and Proposition E2 shows that $N^*s_\infty$ is not constant along this family. In particular we do not compare these onsets with the estimate $N>10.8$ of Proposition E2, which refers to $\varepsilon=1$ (at $N=11$ the onset $\varepsilon^*_{11}=0.958$ is close to $1$ by construction). For $3\le N\le10$ there is no cross-group overlap up to $\varepsilon=1$. The smallest margin is the lowest coupling of group $N+1$ minus the top coupling of group $N$, in the coupling units of the weighted problem. It is $0.293$, at $N=10$ and $\varepsilon=1$, which is a relative size of $4.6\times10^{-3}$ and matches the gap behind Theorem B(i) (centre to centre; the bracket gap there is $4.4\times10^{-3}$). **Proposition G (numerical, against nature).** Let $Z^{\rm gs}(n,l)$ be the lowest atomic number whose ground-state configuration has an electron in the subshell $(n,l)$, taken from the tabulated ground-state configurations of the elements [NIST]. For $1s,2s,2p,3s,3p,4s,3d,4p,5s,4d,5p,6s,5d,4f,6p,7s,6d,5f,7p$ these 19 values are $1,3,5,11,13,19,21,31,37,39,49,55,57,58,81,87,89,91,113$. The anomalous configurations decide four of them: La (57) is the first atom with a $5d$ electron, Ce (58) the first with $4f$, Ac (89) the first with $6d$ and Pa (91) the first with $5f$ (Th, 90, has $6d^2$ and no $5f$). The value 113 for $7p$ is a predicted configuration, not a measured one. With $Z_{\rm first}=(G/1.770682\ldots)^{3/2}$ for these 19 subshells, the Spearman rank correlation with $Z^{\rm gs}$ is 0.9965 and the median relative error is 0.132. A pure counting baseline (Madelung order with capacities $2(2l+1)$) reproduces $Z^{\rm gs}$ with median error 0, so this shows that the model gives first fillings of the right size with no counting input, not that it predicts better than counting. In the sequence of first fillings the step in $Z$ from one subshell to the next equals the capacity of the first when the order is Madelung's (La, Ce, Ac and Pa shift this by one place). So for $N=5$ to $7$ the largest step follows the subshell of highest $l$, and the step after $ns$ is the smallest (2). In the thresholds of the model the largest gap in these groups follows $ns$ (Proposition E). The thresholds therefore see the grouping of the levels, not their capacities.

**Remark H (period bookkeeping, known).** In the Madelung order the chemical period $k$ is the last level of group $k$, which is $ks$, together with group $k+1$ without its $s$ level. Indeed group $N$ consists of the levels with $n+l=N$ and $l\le (N-1)/2$, and its capacity is $c_N=\sum_l 2(2l+1)=2\lfloor (N+1)/2\rfloor^2$, giving $c_N=2,2,8,8,18,18,32,32$ for $N=1,\dots,8$. Period $k$ has $2+(c_{k+1}-2)=c_{k+1}$ places, so the period lengths $2,8,8,18,18,32,32$ are the group sizes shifted by one. This is elementary counting and is known (it is the left-step form of the periodic table of Janet); we state it only to fix the correspondence between groups and periods, and claim nothing new.

**Remark I (numerical, model dependence).** The orderings of Theorems A and B concern the one-body operator with the pure TF field, whose zero-energy threshold is the first filling. The same orderings are obtained when the levels are filled by orbital energy at the actual $Z$ (aufbau with capacities $2(2l+1)$, no self-consistency): the filling $Z$ of $7h$, $11s$, $8g$ is $310$, $314$, $318$, the order of the thresholds. The statement is specific to this field. Rescaling the electron-electron part of the field by $(Z-1)/Z$ (Fermi-Amaldi without tail) moves every threshold by about $3/2$ in $Z$ (one or two in integer $Z$; for example $10s$ moves from 237 to 239, $7h$ from 310 to 311) and changes no order. Fields with the physical $-1/r$ tail (the Latter cutoff, the Fermi-Amaldi field with its tail, and the interpolation between the Latter field and the pure TF field) bind every level, so zero energy is no longer a threshold; filling by orbital energy then reorders levels already at small tail strength (the full Latter tail puts $4p$ (at $Z=21$) before $3d$, the first failure, at $N=5$; the Fermi-Amaldi field with its tail first fails at $N=7$, with $5d$ at $Z=57$ before $4f$ at 61; the weak tails ($0.1$ and $0.25$ of the Latter strength) fail at $N=3$) and moves $11s$ ahead of $7h$ (at $Z$ between about 230 and 290). We therefore claim no robustness of the 0.8 percent and 0.44 percent margins under such corrections, and the result must not be read as a statement about real atoms or about corrected mean fields. The test is numerical, in double precision, for neutral atoms without self-consistency, relativistic or Scott terms. Exchange (the Thomas-Fermi-Dirac field) was not tested. The code is `code/tf_robustness.py`, run by `code/run_all.py --robustness` (documented, not run by default); its log is `code/tf_robustness_aufbau.log` and the crossing test is `code/tf_robustness_cross_0.002.json`.
